🍊 Mission: Think in Ratios

Welcome to a brand-new six weeks! This module is all about ratios and rates — comparing two quantities and using that comparison to solve real problems.

🦥 Ask Arrenia: If a smoothie recipe uses 2 bananas for every 3 cups of orange juice, what does that "2 to 3" actually tell you?
Concept

What Is a Ratio?

A ratio compares two quantities. It can be written three ways, all meaning the same thing:

3 to 4   |   3 : 4   |   3/4

Order matters! "3 sloths to 4 trees" is different from "4 sloths to 3 trees."

🦥 Ask Arrenia: In a classroom with 12 students and 3 tables, what's the ratio of students to tables?
Concept

Rates: A Special Kind of Ratio

A rate compares two quantities with different units — like miles per hour, or price per pound. A unit rate compares to exactly 1 of something.

A car travels 150 miles in 3 hours.
150 miles / 3 hours = 50 miles per hour (unit rate)
🦥 Ask Arrenia: If oranges cost $6 for 3 pounds, what's the unit rate (price per pound)?
Worked Example 1

Build a Ratio Table

A recipe uses 2 cups flour for every 3 cups sugar. Scale it up:

Flour2468
Sugar36912

Every column keeps the same ratio — multiply both numbers by the same scale factor.

🦥 Ask Arrenia: How much sugar do you need for 10 cups of flour, continuing this pattern?
Worked Example 2

Compare Rates to Find the Better Buy

Which is the better deal: 3 pens for $6, or 5 pens for $9?

$6 ÷ 3 pens = $2.00 per pen
$9 ÷ 5 pens = $1.80 per pen
5 pens for $9 is the better buy — a lower price per pen.
🦥 Ask Arrenia: Why is comparing the unit rate more reliable than just comparing the total prices ($6 vs $9)?
Challenge

Real-World Ratio Reasoning

A sloth sanctuary has 15 sloths and 25 volunteers. A second sanctuary has 12 sloths and 18 volunteers. Which sanctuary has more volunteers per sloth?

Sanctuary A: 25 ÷ 15 ≈ 1.67 volunteers per sloth
Sanctuary B: 18 ÷ 12 = 1.5 volunteers per sloth
Sanctuary A has more volunteers per sloth.
🦥 Ask Arrenia: Could you have compared these without finding a unit rate first? Why is the unit rate easier?
