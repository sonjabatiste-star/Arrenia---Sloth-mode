Week 1 · Module 1, Topic 1

🦥 Mission: Crack the Factor Code

Every number is secretly built out of smaller pieces. This week you'll learn to break numbers apart on purpose — with the distributive property — and find the pieces two numbers share, using GCF (greatest common factor) and LCM (least common multiple).

🦥 Ask Arrenia: If you had 24 orange slices and 36 crackers to split into identical snack bags with nothing left over, what's the biggest number of bags you could make?
Concept

The Distributive Property

The distributive property says you can split a multiplication problem into smaller, friendlier pieces and add the results:

a(b + c) = ab + ac
Picture a rectangle that's a tall and (b + c) wide. Slice it into two smaller rectangles — one a × b and one a × c — and the total area doesn't change.
🦥 Ask Arrenia: Why does slicing the rectangle into pieces not change its total area?
Worked Example 1

Break Apart a Hard Multiplication

Find 6 × 47 without a calculator, by rewriting 47 as a friendlier sum:

6 × 47 = 6 × (50 − 3)
= (6 × 50) − (6 × 3)
= 300 − 18
= 282

The same move works with addition: 6 × 47 = 6 × (40 + 7) = 240 + 42 = 282.

Worked Example 2

Greatest Common Factor (GCF)

The GCF of two numbers is the largest number that divides evenly into both. Prime factorization is the fastest way for bigger numbers:

24 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3
36 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3
Shared factors: 2 × 2 × 3 = GCF(24, 36) = 12

That means 24 crackers and 36 orange slices could be split into 12 identical snack bags, each with 2 crackers and 3 slices.

🦥 Ask Arrenia: Can you rewrite 24 + 36 using the distributive property and the GCF, like 12 × (2 + 3)?
Worked Example 3

Least Common Multiple (LCM)

The LCM is the smallest number that both numbers divide into evenly — useful anytime two repeating patterns need to "line up."

Multiples of 8: 8, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48...
Multiples of 12: 12, 24, 36, 48...
First shared multiple: LCM(8, 12) = 24
GCF finds shared factors LCM finds shared multiples

Challenge

Put It Together

A garden club has 18 sunflower seed packets, 30 orange marigold packets, and 42 sloth-friendly fern seedlings. They want the largest number of identical gift bags with none left over.

18 = 2 × 3²
30 = 2 × 3 × 5
42 = 2 × 3 × 7
GCF(18, 30, 42) = 2 × 3 = 6 gift bags

Each bag gets 3 sunflower packets, 5 marigold packets, and 7 fern seedlings.
