Review · 5.4H

Perimeter & Area Refresher

Perimeter is the distance around a shape — add up all the sides. Area is the space inside a shape — for a rectangle, multiply length × width.

8 ft × 5 ft
Perimeter = 8 + 5 + 8 + 5 = 26 ft
Area = 8 × 5 = 40 ft²
🦥 Ask Arrenia: Why does area get measured in "square" units but perimeter doesn't?
Worked Example 1 · Review

A Composite Figure

An L-shaped room is made of a 10 ft × 6 ft rectangle with a 4 ft × 3 ft corner cut out. Find the area.

Big rectangle: 10 × 6 = 60 ft²
Cut-out corner: 4 × 3 = 12 ft²
Total area: 60 − 12 = 48 ft²

Review · 5.6A, 5.6B

Volume of Rectangular Prisms

Volume measures how many unit cubes fit inside a 3D shape, with no gaps or overlaps:

4 in × 3 in × 2 in
V = l × w × h
V = 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 in³

Worked Example 2 · Preview · 6.8B

Area of a Parallelogram

base 9 m, height 4 m

Slice a triangle off one end of a parallelogram and slide it to the other end — it becomes a rectangle with the same base and height!

A = base × height
A = 9 × 4 = 36 m²
🦥 Ask Arrenia: Why does the "slide the triangle over" trick prove the area formula works?
Worked Example 3 · Preview · 6.8B

Area of a Triangle & a Trapezoid

base 10 cm, height 6 cm

Two identical triangles fit together to make a parallelogram — so a triangle's area is always half of base × height:

A = ½ × base × height
A = ½ × 10 × 6 = 30 cm²

A trapezoid's area averages its two parallel sides (called bases) first:

A = ½(b₁ + b₂) × height
Bases 6 in & 10 in, height 4 in → ½(6+10) × 4 = 32 in²
🦥 Ask Arrenia: Why does averaging the two parallel sides make sense for a trapezoid's area?
Preview · 6.8A

Triangle Properties

Two big triangle rules you'll use constantly in 6th grade:

Angle Sum: the three angles inside any triangle always add up to 180°.

Triangle Inequality: the two shorter sides must add up to more than the longest side, or the triangle can't close up.

Example: can 2, 3, and 7 form a triangle? 2 + 3 = 5, which is less than 7 — no, those sides are too short to meet.

Worked Example 4 · Preview · 6.8D

Volume with Rational Dimensions

In 6th grade, prism dimensions won't always be whole numbers — the volume formula works exactly the same way:

A box is 2.5 ft × 3 ft × 4 ft.
V = l × w × h
V = 2.5 × 3 × 4 = 30 ft³
🦥 Ask Arrenia: How is finding this volume similar to the whole-number prism from 5th grade? How is it different?
Challenge

Multi-Step Bridge Problem

A garden bed is shaped like a trapezoid with parallel sides 8 ft and 12 ft, and a height of 5 ft. It will be filled with soil to a depth of 1.5 ft. How much soil (in cubic feet) is needed?

Area of trapezoid: ½(8+12) × 5 = ½(20)(5) = 50 ft²
Volume of soil: 50 × 1.5 = 75 ft³
