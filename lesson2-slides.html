🍊 Mission: Master the Benchmarks

Before you can order a whole mix of fractions and decimals, you need quick mental landmarks to compare against — benchmark fractions: 1/10, 1/4, 1/3, 1/2, and whole numbers.

🦥 Ask Arrenia: Without a calculator, is 5/8 closer to 1/2 or closer to 1?
Concept

Why Benchmarks Work

Benchmarks are "easy" fractions you already know cold. Comparing an unfamiliar fraction to a benchmark is faster than finding a common denominator every time.

1/10 = 0.1 1/4 = 0.25 1/3 ≈ 0.33 1/2 = 0.5
🦥 Ask Arrenia: Which benchmark is 7/8 closest to — and how do you know without dividing?
Worked Example 1

Compare to a Benchmark

Is 5/8 closer to 1/2 or to 1?

1/2 = 4/8, and 1 = 8/8.
5/8 is only 1/8 away from 4/8 (1/2), but 3/8 away from 8/8 (1).
So 5/8 is closer to 1/2.

Worked Example 2

Order Rational Numbers on a Line

Order these from least to greatest: 0.6, 1/3, 7/10, 0.2

Convert everything to decimals: 0.6, 0.333, 0.7, 0.2
Ordered: 0.2, 1/3, 0.6, 7/10
0 0.5 1

Worked Example 3 · Challenge

Rational Numbers Get Trickier

Order from least to greatest: −0.4, 3/4, −1/2, 0.1

Decimals: −0.4, 0.75, −0.5, 0.1
Remember: on a number line, more negative means smaller.
Ordered: −0.5, −0.4, 0.1, 0.75

Real World

Benchmarks in Action

A recipe needs 3/8 cup of orange zest, but your measuring cups only mark 1/4 and 1/2 cup lines. Knowing 3/8 sits right between 1/4 and 1/2 helps you eyeball the fill line accurately — that's benchmark reasoning at work in real life.

Standard Connection

Why This Matters for STAAR

6.4F 6.5C 6.2D — Readiness

6.2D, ordering a set of rational numbers, is a Readiness standard tested across multiple STAAR items every year. Benchmark fluency (6.4F, 6.5C) is the mental-math shortcut that makes those ordering problems fast instead of tedious.
