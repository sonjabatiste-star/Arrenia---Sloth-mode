🌿 Mission: Multiply & Divide Fractions Like a Pro

This week you'll build speed and confidence multiplying and dividing fractions — and understand why the shortcuts actually work, not just memorize them.

🦥 Ask Arrenia: What do you already remember about multiplying two fractions together?
Concept

Multiplying Fractions

Multiply straight across: numerator × numerator, denominator × denominator.

a/b × c/d = (a × c) / (b × d)

A grid model shows why: shading 3/4 of the columns and 2/3 of the rows leaves an overlap that's exactly 2/3 × 3/4 of the whole grid.

🦥 Ask Arrenia: In the grid, how many total squares are shaded twice (the overlap)?
Worked Example 1

Multiply Straight Across

2/3 × 3/4 = (2 × 3) / (3 × 4) = 6/12 = 1/2

Notice 6/12 simplifies to 1/2 — always simplify your final answer.

🦥 Ask Arrenia: Multiply 3/5 × 5/6, then simplify the result.
Concept

Dividing Fractions: Keep-Change-Flip

Dividing by a fraction means asking "how many of these fit?" The reciprocal (flipping the second fraction) turns division into multiplication:

a/b ÷ c/d = a/b × d/c
Keep the first fraction, change ÷ to ×, flip the second fraction.

Why it works: dividing by c/d is the same as asking how many groups of c/d fit into a/b — and multiplying by the reciprocal counts exactly that.

🦥 Ask Arrenia: How many 1/4-cup scoops fit into 3/4 cup? Try it with keep-change-flip: 3/4 ÷ 1/4.
Worked Example 2

Divide Using the Reciprocal

2/3 ÷ 1/4 = 2/3 × 4/1 = (2 × 4) / (3 × 1) = 8/3 = 2 2/3

It makes sense that the answer is bigger than 2/3 — you're asking how many (small) 1/4-size pieces fit into 2/3, so you get several of them.

🦥 Ask Arrenia: Predict whether 5/6 ÷ 2/3 will be bigger or smaller than 1, before solving it.
Challenge

Two-Step Word Problem

A recipe uses 3/4 cup of orange juice per batch. You have 4 1/2 cups of juice. How many full batches can you make?

4 1/2 ÷ 3/4 = 9/2 ÷ 3/4 = 9/2 × 4/3 = 36/6 = 6 full batches
🦥 Ask Arrenia: Why did we convert 4 1/2 into the improper fraction 9/2 before dividing?
Standard Connection

Why This Matters for STAAR

6.3B 6.3E — Readiness 6.2E 6.3A

6.3E, solving problems by multiplying and dividing rational numbers, is a Readiness standard tested heavily on STAAR. Understanding why keep-change-flip works (6.3A) helps you avoid the common mistake of flipping the wrong fraction under pressure.
